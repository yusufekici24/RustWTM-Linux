# RustWTM 0.37.1 release scope and validation

## Scope

Fixes from a visual review of 0.37.0: 474 renders of the Cores page in all
26 languages and of the changed pages in English, Turkish and German, read
by four reviewers. Protocol 19 is unchanged; a 0.37.0 service and a 0.37.1
window work together (the service's reasons are then shown in their 0.37.0
English wording, untranslated).

- **Names.** The plan's reasons, notes and events, and `wtm cores`, name
  entries as the Cores page does: `keep apart 'Game and stream'`, `reserve
  cores 'Audio'`, `keep on cores 'Builds'`, `kept off the cores reserved
  by 'Audio'`. English showed the `config.toml` table names (`separate`,
  `reserve_cores`, `pin`) beside a Kind column with the page's names.
  Validation errors about `config.toml` keep the table names.
- **Mode.** Settings calls the modes *Enforcing* and *Monitoring only*;
  Governor said *Dry-run*, and the Cores, Limits, Kill list and Rules hints
  *Dry-run mode*. These now use the Settings names, in every language.
  The service's event lines keep their `(dry run)` suffix and the About
  page its "Dry-run first" sentence.
- **Compact status bar.** In an 820-pixel window the memory figure was cut
  to a few letters in 12 languages (Tamil showed only `CPU 42% நி…`). The
  summary now drops the word "Memory", then the total (`CPU 42%   8.0 /
  16.0 GiB`, `CPU 42%   8.0 GiB`), before it is cut; *Run new task* and
  *Restart as administrator* are icons below the width where the
  navigation collapses; and the age of current data is left out there
  (stale data still shows its age and the full-width warning). With that,
  every language shows the whole summary in an 820-pixel window, on the
  Cores page and on the fullest bar (Processes, without administrator
  rights).
- **Cores entries.** The edit and delete buttons come first in the table,
  so a translation that widens it cannot push them out of a narrow window.
- **Telegram.** A standard build says that the bot only reports; before,
  only `telegram-control` builds explained it.
- **Docker.** Memory values use the language's decimal separator (they
  showed `160.0 MiB` in Turkish and German).
- **Texts.** The *Hard affinity* and *Include the programs it starts*
  descriptions and the "left alone" line are rewritten in English, and
  the Cores translations corrected per the review: among others Turkish
  (*Çekirdek kuralları*, *Çekirdek tahsis et*, which no longer looks like
  *Ayrı tut*), German *Effizienzkerne*, French *Fixer sur des cœurs*,
  Bengali and Swahili (one word for "reserve", another for "save"), Tamil
  (efficient and performance cores no longer confusable; *hard affinity*),
  Hindi performance cores, Korean rescue core, Japanese entry buttons,
  Polish and Vietnamese efficient cores, Simplified Chinese 核心 and
  「」 quotes, and `0 s` spelled out in six Indic languages.

## Verification

`cargo fmt --check`, `cargo clippy --workspace --all-targets -D warnings`,
`cargo doc` with warnings denied and the workspace tests pass on the
developer host (1219 passed, 23 ignored). `scripts/ci/verify-release.ps1
-Tag v0.37.1` checks the published assets. CI adds 0.37.0 as an upgrade
baseline, with the SHA-256 of its published MSI.

New tests:

- `wtm-gui`: the compact status bar shows an uncut CPU and memory summary
  in all 26 languages, on the Cores page and on the Processes page without
  administrator rights, measured with Windows' own fonts; the shortened
  summaries; the Cores entry buttons inside a 700-pixel page in all 26
  languages; the Telegram note in builds without process control; the
  renamed placement reasons and notes translate.
- `wtm-policy`, `wtm-service`, `wtm-cli`: the existing tests, with the new
  names.

The changed pages (Cores, Governor, Settings, Limits, Kill list,
Containers) were rendered again in English, Turkish, German, French,
Russian, Tamil, Swahili, Japanese, Simplified Chinese and Bengali, light
and dark, wide and compact.

Not covered: the Entries table of the Cores page in a real 820-pixel
window (the renders skip the middle of the page; the test measures it).
